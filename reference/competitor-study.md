# Kikiewa — Competitor & Inspiration Study

Prepared 24 Oct 2026. Sites studied: Trosuit Med Spa (Lagos), ORÍKÌ Spa (Lagos), SkinSpirit (US med spa chain), milk + honey (US spa / medSPA / salon), Hershesons (London salon), Bamford Wellness Spa (UK luxury spa), London College of Beauty Therapy (UK beauty school).

All copy below is paraphrased or written fresh for Kikiewa. Nothing here is a verified Kikiewa fact. Every claim, price and credential still needs the client's sign-off.

---

## 1. Six takeaways that should shape the redesign

1. **Copy milk + honey's structure, not its look.** Its main nav is literally *Spa / medSPA / Salon / Shop / Locations*. Each division gets its own landing page, providers, before-and-after gallery and service menu. That is Kikiewa's three-division model, already proven at premium scale.
2. **The med spa sells "consult first", not "book a treatment".** SkinSpirit and milk + honey both lead medical services with a consultation. Their nav also offers treatments by *concern* (pigmentation, acne and scarring, ageing, texture) alongside treatments by *name*. People search for their problem, not the procedure.
3. **Trust comes from people, not adjectives.** The premium sites show named providers, a "meet our experts" page, review counts and press logos. They don't call themselves world-class. Kikiewa can't invent any of these, so the design needs slots for them, filled with placeholders until verified.
4. **Trosuit is the local bar to beat, and it's good.** It shows prices in naira, opening hours, WhatsApp everywhere, a video tour and one page per treatment. Its weak spots are a crowded mixed-category menu (dental next to hammam), stock-style "why choose us" points, and empty "video story coming soon" placeholders. Kikiewa wins by being more focused, more clinical on the med spa side and more editorial overall.
5. **Premium means restraint.** Bamford uses single-word section labels, lots of whitespace, large photography and calm, unhurried copy. Hershesons positions itself as the opposite of a dated salon: a destination you go to, not a chair you sit in. Neither site shouts.
6. **The Beauty Institute should be designed like a course catalogue, not a spa page.** LCBT shows every course as a card with the same four fields: **Level · Entry requirements · Duration · Fees/funding**, plus "View course". That is exactly what prospective students compare, and Kikiewa's institute page should mirror it.

## 2. Site-by-site notes

### Trosuit Med Spa — Ikeja, Lagos (direct competitor)
- **Structure:** top bar with address, phones and socials, then a mega-menu of 8 numbered categories, a hero with two CTAs (Book an appointment / Chat on WhatsApp) and hours, a scrolling treatment ticker, "step inside" video, signature treatments with duration and ₦ price, "why guests choose us", client stories, visit block with full hours, and a closing CTA.
- **Does well:** prices and durations visible; per-treatment pages (strong for SEO); pre-filled WhatsApp messages with context ("I'd like help choosing a treatment"); medical treatments get a "Book a consultation" CTA instead of "Book this treatment"; descriptive alt text on every image; `en_NG` locale and a local SEO title (service + area + city).
- **Weak:** too many categories make it feel like a mall; generic benefit copy; placeholder testimonials left live; mixed booking paths (its own /book page plus Fresha).
- **Lesson for Kikiewa:** match its practical clarity (prices, hours, WhatsApp, consult CTA) and beat it on focus and polish. **Never ship visible "coming soon" placeholders on a live site.** Hide the section until real content exists.

### ORÍKÌ Spa — multiple Lagos locations
- **Does well:** a separate page per location ("Spa in Lekki", "Spa in Victoria Island"), which is excellent for local search. Its heritage story (the brand name's Yoruba meaning) gives it depth.
- **Weak:** long text-heavy paragraphs, phone-call booking instructions in body copy, an external booking system, and a dated layout.
- **Lesson:** take the per-location SEO pages and the cultural rootedness. Leave the wall-of-text layout.

### milk + honey — US (spa, medSPA, salon)
- **Nav:** Spa (massage, facial, aesthetics, beauty, nails) · medSPA (providers, before + after gallery, treatments, *skin concerns*) · Salon (stylists, before + after, cut/colour/style/extensions) · Shop · Locations. Gift cards, membership and Book sit in the top utility bar.
- **Homepage:** a short calm hero with one CTA, a three-card division split (spa / medSPA / salon) each with a short paragraph, a press logo strip, an "experience" section about pace and atmosphere, a three-point difference statement, reviews with first name and city, a team section, a locations photo grid, products, and a closing "reserve" CTA.
- **Voice:** soft, lowercase, sensory and unhurried. Its CTA invites you to begin a reset rather than "Book now".
- **Lesson:** the closest structural template for Kikiewa. Borrow the division split, per-division providers and galleries, the gift-cards-and-membership utility bar, and the experience section.

### SkinSpirit — US med spa chain
- **Nav:** treatments grouped by type (injectables, lasers and lights, body, facials and peels) **and** by benefit (lifting and firming, tone and texture, acne clearing, hydration and glow). A standalone Consultations link. Experts, education, gift cards, referrals and financing sit under "More".
- **Homepage:** rotating promo hero, treatment shortcuts, "Book a consult", a medical professionals block, locations, skincare shop, newsletter offer, reviews, press logos.
- **Lesson:** the **benefit/concern filter** and **consultation as a product** are the two ideas to steal for the Nitel Medical Spa. Their "Excellence in Education" page is a model for linking the Medical Spa to the Beauty Institute's training credibility, once verified.
- **Avoid:** promo-heavy hero carousels and discount pop-ups. They feel mass-market, not premium.

### Hershesons — London salon
- **Positioning:** frames the traditional salon as outdated and sells "spaces" you'd want to spend time in.
- **Trust:** a Google rating with the review count, short reviews tagged by branch, and a "Meet the experts" grid where each stylist gets one line of personality.
- **Content:** "Hair Stories", an editorial journal with seasonal how-tos (great for SEO).
- **Lesson for Salon & Spa (Ebute):** named stylists with one-line personalities, reviews tagged by branch, and a journal.

### Bamford Wellness Spa — UK
- **Structure:** one wide image, a short philosophy paragraph, single-word category tiles (spa days, treatments, classes, retreats), a split image-and-text block per location, and "What's on" events.
- **Lesson:** this is the reference for *restraint*: uppercase micro-labels, generous whitespace and one idea per section. Spa days and gift cards are sold as gifts, which suits Lagos gifting culture (birthdays, bridal, corporate).

### London College of Beauty Therapy — UK
- **Course cards:** title, a one-sentence summary, then four labelled fields: Level, Entry requirements, Duration, Funding/fees, and "View course". Courses are grouped by discipline, closed intakes are clearly marked, and the progression path is explained in plain words.
- **Lesson:** the template for the Kikiewa Beauty Institute page and its data model.

## 3. Design direction for Kikiewa (premium)

**Overall:** editorial luxury with a clinical wing. Keep the existing system (plum palette, Bodoni Moda + Plus Jakarta Sans, sharp corners, hairlines) and push it further toward Bamford-level restraint.

- **Photography first.** Full-bleed heroes, an asymmetric gallery mosaic, and real interiors (a consultation room, treatment beds, the salon floor, products in use). One curated photoshoot will do more than any layout change. Until then, use placeholders that are honest and quiet, never stock photos of models who aren't clients.
- **Two tones, one system:**
  - *Medical Spa (Nitel):* cool off-white surfaces, more whitespace, ruled tables, label/value credential blocks, no decorative flourishes. Language: assessment, consultation, plan, aftercare.
  - *Salon & Spa (Ebute):* warmer surfaces, larger imagery, softer copy, a gallery-led layout. Language: ritual, unwind, finish, glow.
  - *Beauty Institute:* structured and confident. A course-catalogue grid, progression pathway, intake dates and an "Apply / Book a visit" CTA.
- **Navigation (desktop):** Salon & Spa · Medical Spa · Beauty Institute · Gallery · Locations · Journal, plus a persistent **Book** button and a WhatsApp icon. The utility bar holds gift cards, the two location phones and hours.
- **Mobile:** a sticky bottom bar with Book · WhatsApp · Call, the pattern Trosuit uses well. Most Lagos traffic will be mobile.
- **Motion:** subtle only. Image fade-ins and hover lifts, all disabled under reduced motion. No carousels for core content.
- **Peacock cue:** the logo is a peacock. Use it sparingly, perhaps as a fine-line motif in section dividers or the loader. "Quiet luxury" med spas pair a dark neutral palette with a peacock-gold mark, which validates the restrained direction.

## 4. Copy principles

1. **Lead with the client's outcome or feeling, then the method.** Not "We offer microneedling", but what it helps with, then how it works.
2. **Specific beats superlative.** Ban "best", "world-class", "No. 1" and "guaranteed results". Every premium site that earns trust does it with names, numbers and process.
3. **Consult-first language for anything medical.** "Book a consultation" is the CTA. Results are described as varying from person to person, and suitability is assessed in person.
4. **Short sentences, generous whitespace.** One idea per paragraph, three sentences max on cards.
5. **Rooted, not generic.** Like ORÍKÌ's heritage story, find Kikiewa's real origin or meaning, *from the client*, and tell it once, well.
6. **Practical info is premium too.** Prices (or "from" prices), durations, hours, what to expect, aftercare and cancellation policy. Hiding them reads as evasive, not exclusive.

**Sample lines (draft voice, not facts):**
- Hero, home: *"Three houses of care. One standard."* Subline: the salon and spa in Ebute, the medical spa in Nitel, and the institute training the next generation.
- Medical Spa: *"Every treatment begins with a conversation."*
- Salon & Spa: *"Unhurried care, from the first wash to the final finish."*
- Institute: *"Learn the craft where it's practised."*
- Closing CTA: *"Your appointment, your pace."* Buttons: Book online · Chat on WhatsApp.

## 6. SEO requirements

- **One page per service, treatment, course and location.** Competitors that rank locally (Trosuit, ORÍKÌ) do this, and a single "services" page can't rank for many searches.
- **Titles follow the pattern** Service + area + Lagos | Kikiewa, e.g. "Medical Spa in [Nitel area], Lagos | Kikiewa". Confirm the real area names with the client.
- **Structured data:** `HealthAndBeautyBusiness`/`DaySpa` for Ebute and `MedicalClinic` or `MedicalBusiness` for Nitel (only if licensing supports it), each with address, geo, hours and phone; `Course` for institute programmes; `BreadcrumbList`; `FAQPage` where FAQs are real.
- **Content:** a journal with useful local articles (e.g. skincare in Lagos humidity, preparing for bridal hair and makeup). Two strong articles beat ten thin ones.
- **Technical:** static generation for all marketing pages; `next/image` with AVIF/WebP and correct `sizes` (the gallery will be the LCP risk); a sitemap with every dynamic route; canonical URLs; Open Graph images per division; `en-NG` locale.
- **Google Business Profile** for both locations, matching the site's name, address and phone exactly. This drives local ranking more than anything on the site.

## 7. What to avoid (seen in the research)

- Live placeholder sections ("coming soon" testimonials).
- Promo carousels and discount pop-ups on arrival.
- Mixing unrelated categories into one long menu.
- External booking hand-offs that break the brand experience.
- Unverifiable superlatives ("No. 1", "best in Lagos").
- Text walls with booking instructions buried in paragraphs.

---

## 8. Needed from the client for the premium build

1. Real photography (or approval for a shoot): both interiors, team portraits, treatments in progress (with consent).
2. Team names, roles and **verified** qualifications for the "meet the team" sections.
3. The service/treatment list with prices or "from" prices and durations, and which medical treatments may be advertised.
4. Course list: level, entry requirements, duration, fees, intake dates, certifying body.
5. The brand story: origin of the name "Kikiewa", founder, year.
6. Booking rules: deposit vs full payment, cancellation and refund policy, consult fee.
7. Google review links or real testimonials with permission.
